MODEL GUIDE

How a property
becomes an estimate.

HouseScope turns consistent property information into a fast, evidence-based price estimate. This page shows what happens behind the result and why each step matters.

01

Clean the data

Missing values, unusual domain records, and extreme measurements are handled using the same rules applied during training. Winsorization limits the influence of extreme values without discarding the whole property.

Benefit More stable predictions from imperfect real-world data.
02

Build useful features

The pipeline combines original fields into housing signals such as total floor space, living area, total bathrooms, house age, and other domain-driven measurements.

Benefit The model sees the property as a complete home, not isolated columns.
03

Translate categories

Ordered quality fields keep their meaningful ranking through ordinal encoding. Other categories are converted with one-hot encoding, then numeric features are scaled consistently.

Benefit Words such as quality and condition become signals the model can compare fairly.
04

Generate the estimate

The fitted preprocessing pipeline sends the transformed record to a stacked regression model combining Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, SVR, and Lasso, with Ridge as the final estimator. The predicted log-price is converted back to dollars.

Benefit Several learning patterns are combined into one consistent estimate.

WHY IT MATTERS

From a form or dataset to a decision-ready view

For one property, the result gives an estimated price and an uncertainty range. For a valid CSV, every row is processed with the same saved model so a company can review many properties without retraining the system. Market Segmentation adds a second view by grouping properties into lower, mid-market, and premium patterns.

Important: The model learns from historical Ames Housing patterns. It supports comparison, research, and early valuation decisions; it is not a certified professional appraisal.
